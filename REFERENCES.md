# References

Sources of inspiration and borrowed patterns behind this repo's dotfiles, configs, and agent knowledge base. Vendored third-party files retain their own license headers; the repo `LICENSE` covers original work.

## Dotfiles lineage

- **mathiasbynens/dotfiles** - https://github.com/mathiasbynens/dotfiles - macOS `defaults write` patterns in `setup.sh`, `.gitconfig` alias style, `.wgetrc`, and heritage reference comments. Direct signal: `.gitattributes` (line 3).
- **Solarized (Ethan Schoonover)** - https://ethanschoonover.com/solarized - vim colorscheme (`colorscheme solarized` in `.vimrc`); vendored as `.vim/colors/solarized.vim` under its own MIT header.
- **oh-my-zsh** - https://github.com/ohmyzsh/ohmyzsh - zsh framework loaded by `.zshrc`.

### Context & token efficiency

- **ponytail (DietrichGebert)** - https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail - the seven-rung code efficiency ladder (YAGNI, reuse what already lives here, stdlib, native platform feature, installed dependency, one line, minimum code) as a before-you-write stopping rule, together with the clauses that make it safe, in `references/workflow/code-efficiency-ladder.md`: the ladder-runs-after-understanding carve-out, the edge-case-correctness tiebreak ("lazy means writing less code, not picking the flimsier algorithm"), the never-simplify-away list, and the root-cause-is-the-smaller-diff framing that points the laziness reflex at the root cause instead of away from it. Provenance is worth recording because a smaller downstream repo restates the same ladder: rung 2 was added upstream on 2026-06-22, so any seven-rung form with reuse at position 2 derives from that version or later. Not taken: "ship the lazy version and question it in the same response" - announcing a narrowed deliverable still delivers less than was asked. Also not taken: "non-trivial logic leaves ONE runnable check behind, no frameworks, no fixtures" - a sensible floor for a repo without TDD, but this one has `test-driven-development` and a `make test` gate, so a single-smoke-check minimum would weaken a stronger existing contract. Also borrowed: the `ponytail:` marker for deliberate simplifications, which becomes `// SIMPLIFIED:` in `code-quality` and keeps its two required fields, the ceiling and the upgrade path, plus the observation that a marker naming no trigger is the kind that silently rots; the "thinking is billed too" anti-deliberation clause, which has receipts - its first version lost to a prose-only competitor on wall-clock time, 228s against 136s, because it deliberated about what not to build; the counterfactual-baseline rule from its gain-reporting skill ("NEVER print a per-repo savings number ... the unbuilt version was never written, so there is no real baseline to subtract from in a live repo"), now the counterfactual rule in `writing-skills`; and the practice of pinning safety carve-outs as CI invariants, which is what `LADDER_SAFETY_INVARIANTS` does here. Its measurements are the strongest in this family and remain its own: 12 feature tasks and 6 safety tasks against a real repo, n=4 per cell, one model, LOC as `git diff` added lines, and safety checked deterministically rather than by a judge - the full ladder at -54% LOC and -22% tokens, against -33% and -14% for a paraphrase that also dropped a safety point, 95% against 100%. It publishes where it loses: +26.2% and +38.7% cost on two reasoning models, an aggregate that flips sign by sample on a small local model, and an email-validation regression from 100% to 79% on one non-Claude model, traced to a stdlib helper with an edge-case hole. The tiebreak above is the mitigation it added in response, and on Claude the same audit moves the other way.
- **Caveman (juliusbrussee)** - https://github.com/juliusbrussee/caveman - the "compress speech not thought" framing, byte-for-byte code/command/error preservation, and no-invented-abbreviations rule in the harness Output Formatting; plus the per-agent output-contract pattern (a published digest shape the caller can rely on, and the cost model behind it: a subagent's report is injected into the caller's context verbatim) in `subagent-report-contract` and `dispatching-parallel-agents`. Not taken: caveman pairs its contracts with byte-exact recovery handles backed by SQLite. This repo has no equivalent - reports are not persisted, so the contract carries a completeness rule instead of a recovery path. Also borrowed: the three-tier honesty scale for stating per-harness capability (hook / instruction-note / absent), applied to hook support in `references/workflow/hook-patterns.md` - `.claude/` and `.codex/` are tiered as hook, `.gemini/` as absent, rather than claiming uniform support. Also borrowed: the `HONEST-NUMBERS.md` claim discipline (state how a number was measured or mark it unmeasured, say when the technique loses, and mark an unpublished figure as unpublished rather than estimating) as the Evidence axis in `skill-audit` and the Claims-and-evidence section of `writing-skills`; plus the fail-closed frontmatter validation from its `compile.mjs`, as agent conformance checks rather than a compile step. The borrowing is the discipline, not any figure: caveman's own numbers are self-reported, its headline output-reduction figure is marked unpublished for lack of committed raw pairs, and it documents three A/B runs where the tool was net negative. Scope note added after two later repos measured it against rivals with committed raw data, which the original entry could not do. Chisle's 20-task ledger puts caveman at 80% of a no-tool baseline in total but 98% on the mean task, over baseline on 6 of 20 tasks, with a worst case of 424% on a prompt where it produced 206 lines of speculative implementations for a one-line ask; pooled across the 8 explanation-only tasks it sits at 103%, which is net negative on that whole class of prompt. ponytail's independent agentic run agrees on the code axis, at -20% LOC and +7% tokens. Both are third-party numbers on third-party suites at small n, and Chisle's correctness column is uninstrumented - they are reported here as the parties' claims, not adopted. Nothing above is retracted, because what this repo borrowed was the framing and the claim discipline rather than a tool, and both still hold. The failure mode behind those figures is named though: caveman has no code judgment, which is exactly the gap the ponytail entry above fills. Prose compression and a code ladder are complementary halves, as both upstreams say, and this repo now holds both.
- **RTK Rust Token Killer (rtk-ai)** - https://github.com/rtk-ai/rtk - the command-output-shaping strategies (noise removal, grouping, truncation, dedup) and verbose-to-compact command mapping in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md`; plus the `PreToolUse` advisory-hook mechanism behind `rtk-suggest.sh` (match the command, emit a suggestion, never rewrite) in `hooks/guard-bash-output.sh`, the one-script-many-harnesses shape behind its per-agent init flag (here a `--format claude|codex` flag on one script), and the tool-priority ladder from its `rules/search-strategy.md` in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md`. Not taken: rtk's `rtk-rewrite.sh` rewrites the command before it runs. A lossy filter between the agent and its evidence contradicts this repo's byte-for-byte rule, so the guard here is advisory only - it emits a message, exits 0, and never sets `updatedInput` or `permissionDecision`. Also borrowed: the history-analysis idea behind its discover and session commands (read the harness's own transcripts to report which conventions actually get used) as the adoption report in `skill-audit`. Divergences worth naming, so this is not read as a like-for-like port: rtk runs its analysis as part of normal operation, while this one runs only when a human asks for it, is windowed to 30 days by default, and reports mention counts rather than savings. A mention is a lower bound on use, not a measurement of it, and the report leads with that.
- **Chisle (JayPokale)** - https://github.com/JayPokale/Chisle - the measured case against the unconditional "make it a table" rule, in the Token Density section of `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md` and the harness Communication guidance: its single backfire across 20 tasks was a summarize/compare prompt answered with headed pro/con walls at 173% of a no-tool baseline, rewritten and re-measured at 93% (n=3 per arm). Not taken: its `PostToolUse` compressor is a one-way lossy in-place rewrite with no retention of the original in any store, and it additionally blanks `stderr` on one path; its content guards are a tool allowlist and a bounded regex salvage, with no guard keyed on the content being a diff, a stack trace, or code. Same reason as rtk. Also not taken: "ship the lazy version, question it" and the one-runnable-check minimum, both for the reasons in the ponytail entry. Also borrowed: the compression-suspension list, with resume semantics - security warnings, irreversible-action confirmations, multi-step sequences where fragment order risks misread, compression that creates ambiguity, and a repeated question; the label-a-feature-speculative rule in `skill-audit` and `writing-skills`, which it applies to itself by shipping a dedup tier with zero measured hits on its own corpus, marked speculative and kill-switchable until it earns a number; and the practice of linking published counter-evidence in the same document as the claim it opposes, keeping the part of the objection that survives the fix. Its value here is as a synthesis with receipts rather than an origin - the ladder is ponytail's and the prose discipline is caveman's - and it is the only member of this family that benchmarks its rivals and commits raw per-cell data, which is what makes the caveman note above checkable rather than hearsay. Read its numbers as directional: n=1 per cell above temperature zero, and its own caveat says segment aggregates and backfire patterns are the signal while any single percentage is not. Its correctness column is asserted rather than instrumented - no grader, rubric, or grading prompt is committed anywhere - which matters because "nobody here buys token savings with wrong answers" is load-bearing under every token figure it reports. Its honesty is also unevenly distributed across its own surfaces: the README opens "nothing here is estimated" while one of its figures is labelled estimated, and its comparison page omits the run where it lost billed output outright. Its characterization of ponytail as padding prose describes a version that ponytail measured and fixed within days, and is not repeated here.
- **headroom (headroomlabs-ai)** - https://github.com/headroomlabs-ai/headroom - the per-transformation loss taxonomy in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md`: a *reformat* packs the same information denser and needs no recovery path, an *offload* drops bytes and therefore requires one, and sorting a transformation into the wrong class is how a lossy step comes to be described as safe. Not taken: its proxy delivery mode, for the same reason as rtk's `rtk-rewrite.sh` - a lossy filter between the agent and its evidence contradicts the byte-for-byte rule. The refusal is of the mode and not the project: its library and MCP modes need no proxy, and a lossless-only flag exists. Also not taken: its anonymous beacon, which is on by default and whose own documentation records the check as fail-open, so an unrecognized value uploads rather than staying silent; and its install path, which registers a second tool at user scope. Also borrowed: the round-trip self-check, which is the strongest guarantee in this family precisely because it is a mechanism rather than a promise - a transform claimed lossless ships its inverse and is verified on every call, returning the original unchanged whenever the round trip does not reproduce it or the result is not actually smaller; the measured evidence for read protection, which upgrades an assertion two other repos make into a finding, since lossy-compressing file reads was observed to make agents re-read the same file to recover exact detail and to lose the content outright when recovery failed; the two-indicator rule for error detection, because one keyword false-positives on output that merely mentions the word; the fail-neutral-for-detectors row in the failure-direction table in `references/workflow/hook-patterns.md`, filling a gap the transform-only rule left open; and the holdout that converts a counterfactual estimate into a measurement, now paired with the counterfactual rule in `writing-skills`. Its limitations page is more honest than its README, which is worth naming as the pattern to avoid rather than copy - the README advertises compressing RAG chunks while the limitations page lists RAG contexts as passthrough, and an advertised lossy cap is described in its own source as plumbed but unconsumed. Its "same answers" claim is instrumented, which is rarer than it should be, but thinner than the phrase implies: four benchmarks at n<=100, n=8 on the flagship agent case, an accuracy gate that passes when any one of four metrics passes, no committed result artifacts, and two of four rows at 97% rather than 100%.
- **squeez (claudioemmanuel)** - https://github.com/claudioemmanuel/squeez - the reversible-summarization pattern (persist the full output, cite its path) and the net-win compression gate in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md`. Widened after a second pass, because the original two credits left the rules that make them safe on the table. Not taken: its `PreToolUse` path rewrites the command before it runs, and its own security documentation records the consequence - for commands it wraps, a host `deny` or `ask` permission rule does not fire. Same reason as rtk. Also borrowed: the coupling that closes the hole in reversible summarization - if the original cannot be persisted there is no recovery path, so the compression is refused and the content ships verbatim with a reason, which is the Fail-Open Principle applied to one's own summarizing; the marker cost belonging inside the gate arithmetic rather than after it, a bug it shipped and then fixed, where a call saving 25 tokens emitted a roughly 40-token marker and still reported a win; the compaction floor, since a pointer to content that compaction has evicted leaves the reader a pointer to nothing, which is also why the rule here says persist and cite a path rather than refer back to earlier output; and the measurement methodology in `writing-skills` - adversarial cases where the technique should not win and must fail loudly if it claims one, plus a length-matched naive control, which in its own committed audit shows plain truncation at matched output size beating it on 6 of 22 fixtures. Also borrowed: the BPE substitution audit, which supplies the measurement behind caveman's no-invented-abbreviations rule this repo already held - 33 pairs against two real tokenizers, 13 saving, 13 neutral, and 7 costing, with "because" to "b/c" and "without" to "w/o" each costing a token, concluding that letter substitutions do not beat the tokenizer. That upgrades a borrowed assertion into a sourced one; see the caveman entry above.
- **claude-token-efficient (drona23)** - https://github.com/drona23/claude-token-efficient - the structure-costs-tokens corroboration in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md` ("words drop more consistently than tokens ... markdown and structure tokens partly offset word savings, so word-count benchmarks overstate the token (cost) win"), and the measured clean bill on prose rules that no longer earn their bytes: it found Claude baselines already at 0% incidence of preamble, sycophancy, "as an AI", and smart quotes, and concluded that rules targeting those behaviors carry input cost without changing output. That finding is recorded scoped to the model it was measured on - `.claude/CLAUDE.md` carries none of these rules, while `.codex/AGENTS.md` keeps its own, because a measurement on Claude says nothing about what another harness's model emits, and trimming on this evidence would be the unsourced generalization the Evidence axis forbids. Not taken: its tool-call budget profiles, which cap a task at 20, 30, or 50 calls and instruct wrapping up at 25 - a hard call ceiling truncates investigation, colliding with `completeness-principle` and `debugging-methodology`. Also not taken: its at-scale dollar table, a bare extrapolation with no stated methodology that contradicts its own measured cost deltas a few sections earlier. Also borrowed: the overhead-exceeds-saving-on-short-prompts statement, and the retract-in-place discipline - it kept its retracted 63% headline in the README with a disclaimer while publishing both the harness and the committed raw per-run data that killed it ("the published 63% reduction does not reproduce on the current minimal CLAUDE.md. Real effect ranges from ~-2% (haiku) to -11% (opus, excl. T4)"). The most useful thing here is the shape of the repo rather than any single figure: three mutually inconsistent number sets, with the inconsistency documented rather than reconciled away, and the eval that contradicted the headline shipped alongside it.
- **context-mode (mksglu)** - https://github.com/mksglu/context-mode - the fixed HANDOFF/compaction snapshot schema and progressive-checkpoint cadence in `session-handoff`.
- **Token Savior (Mibayy)** - https://github.com/Mibayy/token-savior - the bash-output-compaction and symbol-first/delta-read navigation guidance in `references/workflow/context-efficiency.md`.
- **claude-token-optimizer (nadimtuhin)** - https://github.com/nadimtuhin/claude-token-optimizer - the tiered always-on-core vs on-demand model behind the working-set context-budget rule.
- **Semantic Anchors (llm-coding)** - https://llm-coding.github.io/Semantic-Anchors - the name-the-methodology-instead-of-explaining rule (and naming BLUF) in the harness Context Efficiency guidance.
- **Skill Codex (skills-directory)** - https://github.com/skills-directory/skill-codex - non-TTY CLI invocation hygiene (`</dev/null`, redirect noise) and the effort-to-timeout heuristic in the harness Bash guidance.
